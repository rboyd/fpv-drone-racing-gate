What changes the result?

Paper size: a 300 mm square is just too wide to get two across the 558.8 mm stock width. A 300 × 250 rectangle yields four per sheet; mixed orientation yields five 300 × 200 rectangles. The true area-only minimum is 13 sheets, but these repeatable layouts need 17–28.

Panel support: smaller bays should flex less, but more rail joints do not automatically make the whole gate stronger. The middle-rib option retains 20 paper pieces while supporting each large panel as two roughly 300 × 450 mm bays. Ribs need light retainers to work under wind from both directions.

Printer size: use rail bodies up to 150 mm long plus compact connectors, within an approximately 170 mm envelope. Several rails can print together; rail piece counts are not print-job counts. One-piece 150 mm panel frames are possible, but duplicate the shared edges and use more material.

Outdoor use: the $0.99 listing describes cardstock and gives no wet-weather rating. Treat it as a replaceable dry-weather insert; framing does not waterproof it. Wind area remains approximately 5.04 m², so existing guying/ballast remains relevant.
